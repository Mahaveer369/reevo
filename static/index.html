    1. Why Berth Allocation (CBAP / C/T-V BACAP / DCBAP) Succeeds Where TSP-ACO Hit a Ceiling

    Clear architectural explanation connecting your TSP-ACO experience to your new Berth Allocation System results.

    Why TSP-ACO Couldn’t Beat Strong Baselines

    • Single Homogeneous Feature (dij): In Euclidean TSP, every edge has only one static number (distance). In problems/tsp_aco/test.ipynb, the Qwen-generated correlation_matrix[j][j] was identically 1.0 on the diagonal, reducing the prior to a static power-law of distance.
    • 2-Opt Basin Collapse: Running 2-opt local search after every ant tour projects mediocre and good tours into the same local minimum, erasing the effect of static priors.

    Why Berth Allocation Is Ideal for LLM Hyper-Heuristics

    • 6 Coupled Spatiotemporal & Resource Dimensions: Every vessel i has arrival time ai, length Li, crane workload ci, crane bounds [qimin, qimax], target berth bi0, and priority weight wi.
    • Proved FIFP Suboptimality (Zou et al. 2025 Fig. 3): Standard First-In-First-Planning (FIFP) creates dead 2D quay slivers (<110m) and starves high-priority Ultra-Large Container Vessels (ULCVs) of quay cranes.
    • Initial-Bound Branch & Bound Synergy (Chen et al. 2026): Chen et al. (2026, Tables 14–16) prove that using an intelligent policy to construct a tight initial Upper Bound (UB-BB) is far more effective and stable than modifying internal B&B pruning rules.

    Dead-Code Algebraic Proof in heuristics_reevo

    In /problems/tsp_aco/test.ipynb (lines 20–42), the ReEvo-generated function computes correlation_matrix = np.corrcoef(edge_attr_norm.T) (O(n3) cost) and sets: 

    heuristic_values[i][j] = np.exp(
    -8 * edge_attr_norm[i][j] * correlation_matrix[j][j]
)

    Because the diagonal of any Pearson correlation matrix satisfies corrjj ≡ 1.0, the entire correlation matrix computation is a mathematical no-op.
