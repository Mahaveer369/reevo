SECTION 2: WHAT MARITIME LOGISTICS PROBLEM & MODELS WE ARE SOLVING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 Operational Context in Deep-Sea Container Terminals
In modern maritime container terminals (e.g., Shanghai Yangshan, Rotterdam,
Singapore, Ningbo), the shoreline quay is the single most expensive capital
asset. Every day, heterogeneous container vessels — from 110m feeder vessels to
385m Ultra-Large Container Vessels (ULCVs) — arrive dynamically at the port.
Terminal dispatchers must simultaneously decide:
  (a) WHERE along the continuous shoreline [0, L] each ship moors (b_i),
  (b) WHEN each ship starts and finishes berthing (t_{s,i}, t_{e,i}), and
  (c) HOW MANY rail-mounted Quay Cranes (QCs) q_{i,tau} serve ship i during
      each hour tau without exceeding total shoreline crane capacity |Q|.

If ships are poorly scheduled:
  - Ships queue at anchorage -> massive waiting cost, vessel fuel burn, and
    missed shipping-alliance schedules.
  - Ships moor far from their preferred yard container stacks (b_i^0) ->
    internal yard trucks travel hundreds of extra meters per container,
    slowing handling speed (position-dependent handling time).
  - Uncoordinated berthing leaves unusable "dead quay slivers" (< 110m) where
    no incoming ship can physically fit.
  - Under dynamic arrivals, fixed-interval replanning reacts too slowly, while
    unconstrained replanning illegally tries to move ships already moored and
    actively discharging containers.

2.2 The Three Mathematical Models Unified in Our Framework (`model.py`)
Our codebase unifies THREE standard maritime optimization formulations in one
exact mathematical engine:

  Model A — CBAP (Continuous Berth Allocation Problem with Position-Dependent
            Handling Times)
    * References: Guan & Cheung (2004), Lee et al. (2010), Martin-Iradi et al. (2024)
    * Benchmark : `martin24_cbap_25` (N=25 vessels, L=1000m)
    * Key physics: Handling duration increases linearly with berth offset from
      the vessel's preferred yard position b_i^0:
          h_i(b_i) = (1 + beta_pos * |b_i - b_i^0|) * h_i^0

  Model B — C/T-V BACAP (Continuous Berth Allocation & Time-Variant Quay Crane
            Assignment Problem)
    * References: Xu et al. (IEEE TITS 2025), Zou et al. (IEEE TETCI 2025)
    * Benchmarks: `xu25_bacap_20` (N=20, L=1000m, |Q|=20),
                  `xu25_bacap_30` (N=30, L=1200m, |Q|=24),
                  `xu25_bacap_40` (N=40, L=1500m, |Q|=30)
    * Key physics:
      - Continuous berth coordinate b_i with 10% vessel-length safety clearance:
            clearance(i, j) = ceil_step(0.10 * max(L_i, L_j))
      - Time-variant hourly crane allocation q_{i,tau} in [q_i^min, q_i^max]
      - Total crane-hour workload completion: sum_{tau} q_{i,tau} >= c_i
      - Shoreline crane capacity at every hour tau: sum_{i active at tau} q_{i,tau} <= |Q|

  Model C — DCBAP (Event-Driven Rolling-Horizon Dynamic Continuous BAP)
    * References: Chen et al. (Ocean Engineering 2026, Table 10 & Sec. 4.3)
    * Benchmarks: `chen26_table10` (exact 10-vessel case from Table 10, L=1200m),
                  `chen26_n20` (N=20, L=1200m), `chen26_n30` (N=30, L=1200m)
    * Key physics: Four dynamic vessel operational statuses at every event time:
      - Status 0: Already berthed & working -> LOCKED (b_i, t_{s,i}, qc_profile immutable)
      - Status 1: Already departed -> Completed
      - Status 2: Scheduled but not yet berthed -> Eligible for re-optimization
      - Status 3: Newly arrived dynamic vessel -> Inserted into active horizon

2.3 Unified Objective Function & Analytical Lower Bound
Every solver optimizes the exact weighted port operations objective:

    min Z = sum_{i=1..N} w_i * [ alpha_wait   * (t_{s,i} - a_i)
                               + alpha_handle * (t_{e,i} - t_{s,i})
                               + beta_dev     * |b_i - b_i^0| ]

And every schedule is benchmarked against the Analytical Lower Bound (LB)
derived from Equation (19) of Chen et al. (2026) and Guan & Cheung (2004):
each unlocked vessel is evaluated at its conflict-free ideal berth b_i^0,
earliest arrival a_i, and fastest handling speed (ceil(c_i / q_i^max) for
C/T-V BACAP or ideal h_i^0 for CBAP). No feasible schedule can ever score below LB.

SECTION 4: HOW WE DESIGNED OUR OWN SOLVER (`solve_reevo_vrpagent_bap`)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Every single paper in Section 3 has one structural blind spot:
  - HMA (Xu 2025) relies on generic order-crossover that struggles to break
    coupled spatiotemporal clusters of delayed ships.
  - ALNS (Martin-Iradi 2024) scores candidate insertions purely by immediate
    single-vessel cost, ignoring future quay fragmentation (< 110m dead slivers).
  - I2HCSO (Zou 2025) lacks exact branch-and-bound pruning on congested windows.
  - UB-BB (Chen 2026) depends on a single constructive upper bound; if that
    upper bound is weak, B&B wastes nodes without reaching the best basin.

We designed `solve_reevo_vrpagent_bap` (100% stateless & standalone in
`problems/berth_allocation/solvers.py`) as a 4-Phase Unified Architecture:

  PHASE 1 — Scale-Invariant ReEvo Priority Portfolio + Proposition 3.1 Vertex
            3-Stage QC Decoding
    * Normalizes vessel weights w_norm = w_i / mean(w) so the priority key
        k_i = a_i - 0.85 * w_norm - 0.0022 * L_i - 0.08 * (c_i / q_i^max)
      is mathematically scale-invariant across different terminal cost scales
      (fixing arrival-wave inversion when weights range from [1, 2] vs [1, 10]).
    * Decodes 6 complementary priority orderings strictly on Lee et al. (2010)
      Proposition 3.1 exact vertices (`dense_grid=False`) through Xu et al. (2025)
      3-Stage Time-Variant QC + Horizontal Berth Offset Rectification.
    * Guided by our ReEvo Spatiotemporal Hole-Tightness Scorer:
        score(i, b_i, t_s, t_e) = base_cost(i) + dead_sliver_penalty - flush_corner_bonus
      where:
        - dead_sliver_penalty penalizes leaving unusable gaps in (0, 110m)
          against quay walls or temporally overlapping ships;
        - flush_corner_bonus rewards placing ships flush (<= 25m gap) against
          concurrent neighbors to preserve contiguous shoreline for ULCVs.

  PHASE 2 — Bottleneck-Driven Priority Elevation (VRPAgent Targeted Search)
    * Computes each vessel's exact excess cost above its Analytical Lower Bound
      Eq. (19) and selects the top 4 bottleneck vessels.
    * Tests promoting each bottleneck vessel earlier by 1..3 sequence slots and
      re-decoding through the 3-Stage QC Decoder, accepting strict improvements.

  PHASE 3 — Adaptive Large Neighborhood Search (ALNS Destroy/Repair Portfolio)
    * Runs 6 destroy-repair iterations pairing (`cost_time`, `shaw`,
      `spatiotemporal_cluster`) destroy operators with (`regret_2`,
      `strict_packing`) repair operators, alternating between our ReEvo
      hole-tightness scorer and exact objective scoring under Simulated Annealing.

  PHASE 4 — Dual-Scorer TGLS + Exact UB-BB Window Polish
    * Runs Target-Guided Local Search (ejection-reinsertion + pairwise exchange
      from Xu 2025 & Guan & Cheung 2004) under both scorers.
    * Feeds the tightened Upper Bound into Chen et al. (2026) Depth-First
      Branch-and-Bound (`_branch_and_bound_window_polish`, window=4, max_nodes=18)
      over the highest excess-cost contiguous window, pruned by Analytical LB Eq. (19).
